As announced on svgmap.org, the SVGMap project is entering a new stage.

After nearly three decades of development, SVGMap is evolving into **Hyper-Layered Map**, and the community around it is moving to **hyperlayering.org**. This is not a replacement for SVGMap, but a shift in focus toward the architectural concept that has been at the heart of the project.

SVGMap began as an open approach to building rich, user-driven Web maps without relying on centralized control. Its underlying ideas include decentralized architecture, client-centric interoperability, and the ability to combine independent Web resources as map contents.

At the center of these ideas has always been **Hyper-Layering Architecture (HLA)**.

For most of its history, SVGMap was developed largely through the contribution of a single individual. Over time, however, open-source implementations were created, the technology was used in practical systems, and other people and organizations became involved. More recently, with the support and contributions of **WebDINO Japan**, the project has begun to reach a stage where it can develop as a community rather than primarily as an individual effort.

This is the main reason we have decided to put **Hyper-Layering** at the center of the community's identity and naming.

The name SVGMap originally came from the underlying document format, **Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG)**. SVG was important to the development of the project, but the more fundamental idea was that independent Web resources should be able to retain their independence while being combined through the Web. In the context of geographic information, this means that different sources can be brought together by users with geographic space providing a common context. We call this approach **Hyper-Layering**.

The World Wide Web itself began from a similar principle. Independent documents could be connected through hyperlinks without requiring them to be collected into a single system. As the Web evolved, this principle enabled people, organizations, and information resources to participate while retaining a significant degree of independence.

We believe that Hyper-Layering can extend this principle into the geographic information space. Rather than requiring geographic information from different sources to be collected and integrated into a centralized system, the sources can remain independent while becoming interoperable through the Web.

In this sense, HLA is more than an approach to Web mapping. It is an attempt to extend some of the fundamental principles of the Web to geographic information, and ultimately to contribute to the development of a more open and decentralized social infrastructure for humanity.

This is a long-term vision, and there is still much work to be done. SVGMap has been one way of exploring this idea for nearly three decades. With hyperlayering.org, we would like to continue that exploration as a community.

SVGMap, **svgmapjs**, **Layers as Web Apps (LaWA)**, and the technologies built around them will continue to evolve. From now on, however, **Hyper-Layering** will be the name that brings these activities together.

Welcome to **hyperlayering.org**. We look forward to continuing this journey with the community.
